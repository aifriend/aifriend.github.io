MindForge Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 12, 2026

TL;DR

MindForge collects no data: the app itself sends nothing about you to the developer. Your progress stays on your device. From version 2.0, if you leave iCloud sync on, a copy also goes to your own iCloud account, where Apple stores it and the developer cannot read it. Game Center leaderboards also arrive with version 2.0 and stay off unless you turn them on. The one optional purchase is handled by Apple's App Store, not by MindForge.

Data Collection

MindForge does not collect personal data, and the app sends the developer none. The app's own code makes no network requests, and the game plays fully offline. Data leaves your device only through Apple's own services, as described under Purchases, Ratings, iCloud Sync and Game Center below.

Two things can reach the developer through Apple rather than through the app. A rating or review you post on the App Store is visible to the developer, as it is for every app. If you turned on Share With App Developers in the iOS Settings app (Privacy & Security, Analytics & Improvements), Apple shares crash reports and usage statistics about the app with the developer under Apple's privacy policy; MindForge adds nothing of its own to them.

Version 2.0 adds several features that this policy describes, each marked "from version 2.0". Earlier versions have none of them.

Purchases

Digit Span is free. From version 2.0 the memory techniques are free too, and each other mode can be played once for free. The other modes are unlocked by one optional in-app purchase, which Apple processes through the App Store using your Apple Account. MindForge never sees your payment details, your name, or your Apple Account. It only receives Apple's confirmation that the purchase was made, which stays on your device so the modes stay unlocked. When the app starts, it asks the App Store for the purchase's price in your country. Buying the purchase or restoring it also needs a connection to the App Store; playing does not. Apple's handling of purchases is covered by Apple's own privacy policy.

Ratings

From version 2.0, after a session that went well, the app may show Apple's standard request to rate MindForge, at most once per app version and never within 60 days of the previous request. The app is not told whether you rated it or what you chose. A rating or review you leave goes to the App Store under Apple's privacy policy, and a written review is shown publicly with your App Store nickname. The app keeps on the device only the app version and the date of its last request.

Local Storage

All game progress (XP, level, best scores, difficulty and sessions played) is stored on your device, inside the app's own storage. From version 2.0, so is a record of each finished session: its date, the exercise, the score, the number of correct answers out of the rounds played, the difficulty before and after, how long it took, whether it was part of the daily workout and, for Go / No-Go, the average reaction time. Memory technique practices are recorded in the same way.

Your settings are kept on the device too, including, from version 2.0, the iCloud and Game Center switches. From version 2.0, so are which free sessions you have used, the names you give to the stops of your memory palace, today's workout plan and which of its exercises you have finished, and when the app last asked for a rating or wrote to iCloud.

From version 2.0, a short summary (your training days this week, your weekly goal, your level and today's workout progress) is shared with the MindForge widget through a folder on the same device. You can place the widget on the Home Screen or the Lock Screen; on the Lock Screen it can be seen without unlocking the device.

None of this is sent to the developer or to a server of ours. Part of it leaves the device only through Apple: the iCloud copy, which is on by default, and the Game Center scores, which stay off unless you turn them on, both described below. Separately, iOS includes the app's files and settings, among them your full session history and your memory palace names, in the backups it makes of your device to iCloud or to a computer, as it does for every app.

iCloud Sync

From version 2.0, if iCloud sync is on (Settings, Sync Progress with iCloud; on by default), a copy of your progress is kept in iCloud's key-value storage in your own Apple Account, so it survives reinstalling the app and appears on your other devices signed in to the same account. The copy holds your XP, sessions played, the difficulty and best score of each exercise, your 400 most recent session records (memory technique practices included), the time it was last written and the date of the last Reset All Progress made on any of your devices while iCloud sync was on. Your settings, the purchase confirmation and the names of your memory palace stops are not part of it. Apple stores this copy under Apple's privacy policy; the developer cannot read it. Turn the switch off to stop syncing; the copy already in iCloud stays there.

Game Center

From version 2.0, Game Center leaderboards are available and off by default. If you turn them on (Settings, Game Center Leaderboards), you sign in to Apple's Game Center, and the score of each exercise session you finish while signed in is sent to the leaderboard of its exercise, where other Game Center players, such as your friends, can see it next to your Game Center nickname. Practice runs, scores of zero and memory technique practices are not sent, and MindForge sends Game Center nothing but these scores. Turn the switch off to stop sending scores; scores already sent stay with Apple. Game Center is Apple's service, covered by Apple's privacy policy.

Third-Party Services

MindForge does not use any third-party analytics, advertising, tracking, crash reporting, AI services or cloud services. No third-party SDKs are integrated. The only online services involved are Apple's own: the App Store for the purchase and for ratings and, from version 2.0, iCloud, which is used unless you turn iCloud sync off, and Game Center, which is used only if you turn it on.

Tracking

MindForge does not track users across apps or websites. No advertising identifiers, device fingerprints, or behavioral data are collected.

Children's Privacy

MindForge is rated 4+. The app does not collect personal information from anyone, children included. Game Center leaderboards, the only feature that sends anything about a player to other people, are off by default, and a parent can limit Game Center on a child's device in Screen Time, under Content & Privacy Restrictions. If you believe a child has emailed us personal information, write to us and we will delete it.

Your Rights

The app sends the developer no personal data, so the only information about you that we may hold is an email you choose to send us; the Contact section explains how we handle it. Your progress is under your control.

Settings, Reset All Progress deletes your XP, level, best scores, difficulty and sessions played on this device and, from version 2.0, your session records. Your settings and the full-game purchase stay. From version 2.0, so do the free sessions you have used and your memory palace names, and today's workout keeps the exercises you already finished until the next day.

From version 2.0, when iCloud sync is on, the reset also empties your iCloud copy, and each of your other devices with sync on deletes its progress from before the reset when it next reads iCloud; sessions played on it after the reset stay. A device that was offline does this once it receives the reset. If it sent its older progress to iCloud before receiving the reset, your other devices take in only the sessions it played after the reset, and the reset reaches it the next time MindForge is open on a device that has already applied the reset.

From version 2.0, when iCloud sync is off, the reset clears only this device. Your iCloud copy and your other devices keep their progress, and nothing is sent to them. If you turn sync back on later, this device first reads the copy in iCloud and combines it with its own, so the progress stored there comes back to this device; if one of your other devices was reset with sync on in the meantime, this device instead deletes its progress from before that reset and keeps what was played after it. This also applies to a device that had sync off when another device was reset, even if it was never reset itself. To clear this device and the iCloud copy together, reset while sync is on.

Deleting the app removes everything it stored on the device. Before version 2.0, that deletes your progress for good. From version 2.0, it does not remove the copy in iCloud. After a reinstall, iCloud sync is on again even if you had turned it off, and the app reads that copy back, with your 400 most recent session records; older records do not come back. To empty the iCloud copy after deleting the app, reinstall it, leave sync on and use Reset All Progress; the copy then holds no progress, only the starting difficulty of each exercise, the time it was written, the date of the reset and, if a device's clock was wrong, the random identifiers of sessions the reset deleted. Scores already sent to Game Center are held by Apple, and Apple's privacy policy explains how to manage them.
